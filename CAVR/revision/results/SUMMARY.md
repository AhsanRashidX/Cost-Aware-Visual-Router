# CAVR revision: results summary

Generated by `make_summary.py` from `results/*.json`. No number below was typed by hand.

## Setup

- **Hardware:** 1× NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 (24.0 GB, power limit 450 W), driver 572.61, CUDA 12.8, 32 CPU threads, 63.9 GB RAM, Windows-10-10.0.26200-SP0.
- **Data:** DocVQA + InfographicVQA validation, 8150 questions over 1786 unique pages (`data/manifest.json`).
- **Paths:** one generator (Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, greedy) for all three:
  - text: BM25 top-3 pages of docTR OCR text
  - visual: ColPali v1.3 top-1 page image, ≤ 1280 visual tokens
  - hybrid: both
- **Metrics:** ANLS, exact match, and costs measured on this GPU: GPU-seconds, NVML energy, wall latency.
- **Evaluation:** 5 seeds; each seed is a new page-grouped 60/20/20 split, so no page appears in two splits.

## Bottom line: the claim the evidence supports

- **Content-aware routing (CAVR-CE) uses about 29% less GPU compute than always-hybrid, at a small ANLS cost.**
  - Quality: ANLS 0.557 vs 0.566 for always-hybrid.
  - Compute: 0.716 vs 1.009 GPU-s per query, including ColPali and the router's own overhead.
  - Choice of λ: picked on validation data.
- **Against always-hybrid:** ANLS is lower on 4 of 5 seeds (mean -0.008); the 95% interval excludes zero on 2 of 5 seeds. On exact match the difference is not significant on 5 of 5 seeds (McNemar).
- **Against always-visual:** CAVR-CE is significantly better on 5 of 5 seeds (McNemar on exact match), but uses 15% more GPU.
- **At serving scale** (simulated, 100 QPS at p95 ≤ 2 s, mean over seeds): CAVR-CE needs 72.4 ± 3.9 GPUs against 106.0 ± 0.6 for always-hybrid.
- **What is not supported:**
  - the submitted 86.2% routing accuracy, because its labels don't match outcomes;
  - $0.00019 per query, because it was never measured;
  - the premise that the text path is cheap with a shared generator.
- **Open problem:** a per-query oracle shows much larger headroom (ANLS 0.630 at 0.486 GPU-s). Predicting per-query success is what limits routing.

**How to read this honestly:**
- **Default λ.** CAVR sends most queries to visual and is statistically the same as always-visual (McNemar p per seed: 1, 1, 0.8, 0.13, 1). It is significantly worse than always-hybrid on EM (p: 0.00027, 0.0034, 0.26, 0.013, 0.00044), at 36% lower GPU cost.
- **Lower λ.** CAVR + retrieval scores at λ = 0.05 reaches ANLS 0.563 at 0.829 GPU-s, against always-hybrid's 0.566 at 1.009. That is 18% less compute at a change of -0.002 ANLS.
- **Headroom.** The gap to the oracle (0.630 at 0.486 GPU-s) is large. Question text and retrieval scores predict only part of it.

### Content-aware router (CAVR-CE)

CAVR-CE is the Phase 1 router. Phase 0 probes are in `results/phase0/VERDICT.md`.

**How it works**
- An mpnet cross-encoder reads the question plus the best-matching OCR passages from BM25's pages and ColPali's top page.
- It also takes tabular inputs: retrieval scores, content-match features and ColPali per-token match statistics.
- It predicts each path's ANLS. λ is chosen per seed on validation data as the cheapest setting within 0.005 ANLS of hybrid.

**Cost and accuracy**
- Router overhead: 24.8 ms and 1.74 J per query, plus ColPali for every query. Both are included in all numbers.
- Success-prediction AUC: 0.842, against 0.839 for gradient boosting on the same tabular features. The pre-set target was 0.87, so it was missed.
- Learning curve (2 seeds): AUC goes from 0.826 at 25% of the training data to 0.841 at 100%, about +0.007 per doubling and flattening. More labelled data is unlikely to reach 0.87.

**Results per seed**
- GPU saving vs always-hybrid: 27%, 30%, 27%, 25%, 36%.
- McNemar p vs hybrid: 0.12, 0.11, 0.56, 0.74, 0.098.
- McNemar p vs visual: 0.004, 0.029, 0.0018, 0.029, 0.0046.

**Escalation on CAVR-CE** (ColPali margin, τ = 0.5): ANLS 0.557 → 0.564, GPU-s 0.716 → 0.856, with 34% of queries escalated. Escalation trades back part of the saving to close the remaining quality gap. The full τ sweep is in `paper_out/figures/escalation.png`.

### Escalation (step 7, CAVR base, mean over seeds)

- **Best signal:** the ColPali score margin, applied before generation (re-route to hybrid). At τ = 0.7 it gives ANLS 0.569 at 0.899 GPU-s, p95 1.37 s, with 64% of queries escalated.
- **Answer log-prob signals** need the first answer before deciding, so they add latency.
  - Serial: p95 1.98 s.
  - Speculative (hybrid launched alongside the routed path, assuming 2 concurrent workers): p95 falls to 1.41 s, but GPU cost rises to 1.55 GPU-s.

- Batch-1 latency: 11.7 ms on the GPU, 25.0 ms on the CPU.

**ColPali index at 100k pages** (24.6 GB in bf16) does not fit on one 24 GB GPU:
- Streamed from host RAM: 1044 ms per query.
- 2×2 pooled, on the GPU: 6.3 GB and 10.3 ms per query, but R@1 on the real corpus drops from 0.577 to 0.525.
- Sharded over 2 GPUs, emulated on the 4090 (shards timed one after another): 19.8 ms per query.
- Building the index takes about 1.7 GPU-hours at 16.2 pages/s.

**Multi-GPU index sharding** (measured on 3× Tesla P40, retrieval only; fp16 index, fp32 scoring, host merge of per-shard top-10):
- Top-1 agreement with one GPU: 99.8% exact, 100% counting exact score ties (500 questions).
- 50k pages, p50: 327 / 164 / 109 ms on 1 / 2 / 3 GPUs (efficiency 99.8% on 3). From 50k pages up, dispatch + merge add at most 0.6 ms.
- Batch-64 throughput efficiency ≥ 99.7% from 10k pages up. Small corpora gain less at batch 1 (1k pages: ×2.51 on 3 GPUs).
- Weak scaling: 150k pages (36.9 GiB, more than one GPU holds) on 3 GPUs in 328 ms, the same as 50k pages on one GPU (99.9% efficiency).
- Node energy per query at 50k pages: 28.4 J on 1 GPU → 17.8 J on 3. The drop is the idle draw of unused GPUs (≈51 W each); active energy is 17.8 J per query regardless of GPU count.
- One P40 is 17× slower than the 4090 on the 50k search, so absolute times don't transfer; the scaling with GPU count does.

**Second sharding node** (measured on 2× NVIDIA A10, same benchmark, fp16 scoring):
- Top-1 agreement with one GPU: 99.8% exact, 100% counting exact score ties.
- 50k pages, p50: 41.1 / 21.0 ms on 1 / 2 GPUs (efficiency 97.6%); dispatch + merge ≤ 0.5 ms from 50k pages up; batch-64 throughput efficiency ≥ 98.6% from 10k pages up.
- Weak scaling: 100k pages (24.6 GiB) on 2 GPUs in 41.4 ms vs 50k on one (99.2%). Largest corpus on 2 GPUs: 120k pages.
- One A10 is 8.0× faster than one P40 on the 50k search, with 5.6× less active energy per query (3.19 J); it is 2.1× slower than the 4090. Unused GPUs in the run hold a CUDA context (≈57 W each).

**Index in host memory** (2× NVIDIA A10, cudaHostRegister'd host RAM streamed over PCIe, fp16 scoring):
- Streamed top-1 = GPU-resident top-1 (page and score) for all 100 check queries on 100k pages.
- Copy only: 24.2 GB/s on 1 GPU, 41.0 GB/s on 2 (85% of 2× one GPU; cause not identified: PCIe bridges (PXB), NUMA placement uncontrolled).
- 1,000,000 pages (246 GiB): p50 10.9 s on 1 GPU, 6.4 s on 2 (PCIe-bound); batch 64: 2.25 / 4.50 q/s = 0.44 GPU-s per query (compute-bound from batch ≈25).
- vs GPU-resident at 100k pages on 2 GPUs: 94% of the batch-64 throughput, 6.7 vs 6.4 J per query.
- A GPU-resident 1M-page index would need 16 A10s (projected p50 51 ms, not measured).

## Limitations to state in the paper

- **One consumer GPU (RTX 4090) under Windows WDDM.** Caps on visual tokens and OCR text length keep memory below the point where the driver spills to system RAM. Batch sizes that came near that point are excluded.
- **Generation uses HF transformers `generate`, not a serving engine** (vLLM/TGI), so batching gains are modest. Absolute capacity numbers are therefore pessimistic. The relative comparison between policies is the main result.
- **The load test is a discrete-event simulation** driven by measured per-query service times and measured batching curves, not live traffic.
- **10k–100k-page indexes repeat the real page embeddings.** That is valid for latency and memory, not for retrieval quality at that scale.
- **Multi-GPU sharding was measured on two server nodes (3× Tesla P40, 2× NVIDIA A10), for retrieval only.** Absolute times don't transfer to the 4090, and the load test doesn't include sharded retrieval. On the 4090 itself, sharding is emulated.
- **Speculative-escalation latency assumes two concurrent workers.**
- **Retrieval searches each dataset's own page pool.** DocVQA questions are often generic, so page recall is low and caps every policy (the 35% of queries that fail on every path).
